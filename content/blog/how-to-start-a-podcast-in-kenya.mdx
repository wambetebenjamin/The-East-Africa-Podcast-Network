Starting a podcast in Kenya has never been cheaper — or more competitive. This is the guide we wish existed when we launched the network: honest numbers, no affiliate fluff.

## Budget honestly

You do not need a studio. You need a quiet room and a decent microphone. Here is what we recommend to every beginner who emails us:

- **Entry level (KES 8,000–15,000):** a USB condenser mic, a pop filter, and foam panels on the walls behind you.
- **Serious hobbyist (KES 25,000–40,000):** a dynamic XLR mic, a two-channel interface, and closed-back headphones.
- **Semi-pro (KES 70,000+):** two matched mics for interviews, a boom arm each, and acoustic treatment that actually works.

The biggest mistake first-timers make is buying an expensive mic and ignoring the room. A KES 90,000 microphone in an echoey sitting room sounds worse than a KES 8,000 mic in a carpeted bedroom.

## Record in the right format

Record at 48kHz, 24-bit, WAV. Storage is cheap; bad audio is forever. Leave 2 seconds of silence at the start of every take — noise reduction tools need a clean sample.

> Rule of thumb: if you can hear the fridge, the mic can hear the fridge.

## Edit for the ear, not the eye

Cut the ums that interrupt a thought, not every single one. Listeners forgive humanity; they do not forgive tedium. Aim for a pace that feels slightly slow as you edit — playback always feels faster to the listener.

## Distribution

Upload once, distribute everywhere: Spotify, Apple Podcasts, YouTube Music and the smaller apps all ingest from a single RSS feed. Reserve your show name on every platform the day you decide to launch, even before episode one.

## Consistency beats perfection

Publish weekly, same day, same time, for at least ten episodes before you judge the numbers. The East African podcast audience is loyal but slow to adopt — the shows that win are the ones that show up.
