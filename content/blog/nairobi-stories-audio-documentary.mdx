Something changed on Nairobi's matatu routes around 2021. Where riders used to argue football, they started nodding along to headphones. Data had become cheap enough — and podcasts good enough — that the commute turned into listening time.

## The commute economy

Nairobi's average one-way commute is 47 minutes. Multiply that by two, five days a week, and you get roughly eight hours of captive, high-attention listening per commuter per week. Radio understood this decades ago. Podcasts are only now catching up.

## The archive advantage

East Africa is sitting on one of the richest audio archives on the continent: decades of state radio reels, local-language music, and court and parliament recordings that are public by law. Producers who dig find gold. Our own true-crime show built an entire episode from a 2003 community baraza tape a teacher recorded in Nakuru.

## Languages matter

Swahili-first podcasts are growing three times faster than English-first ones in our network's numbers. Sheng content travels furthest of all — but it ages fastest too. Choose deliberately.
