Three of our ten shows — including two of the most-downloaded — are recorded in ordinary apartments. Here is exactly how.

## The duvet booth

The cheapest vocal booth in Nairobi is a wardrobe full of clothes and a duvet. Record facing into it. The fabric kills the slap-back echo that marks amateur audio. Total cost: zero.

## The night shift

Nairobi is quietest between 11 p.m. and 5 a.m. — and so are its power spikes. Record then if you can. If you cannot, at least avoid the hour the neighbourhood generator tests itself.

## Gikomba acoustics

Foam panels are expensive; second-hand duvets and curtains are not. Hang heavy fabric on the wall behind your mic (the wall you speak *towards*) and behind your head. Two layers of fabric beat one layer of foam.

## The chain that matters

Microphone → interface → recording software. Nothing else. Every plugin, every "AI enhancer", every magic button is a repair tool for a problem you should fix in the room instead.

## Test like a pro

Record 30 seconds, listen on earphones, then on your phone speaker, then in a matatu if you are brave. Your listeners use all three. If it survives the matatu test, ship it.
